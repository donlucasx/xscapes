xscapes Brand Guidelines

The identity is a block cursor over the x. This page is the whole rulebook: the mark and its geometry, the colours and where each one may go, the type, the voice, and the files in the repository.

The idea

The oldest waiting screen in computing is a blinking block cursor. xscapes is what that cursor turns into while an agent works. So the mark is the terminal's own cursor, drawn over the x by reverse video, and any terminal can print it without an image:

printf '\e[7mx\e[0mscapes'

Four rules

They come from the product, not from taste. Every decision below follows from one of them.

Every colour is an xterm-256 index, 16 to 255.

The product never emits below 16, because those sixteen are the only colours a terminal profile can repaint. The brand keeps the rule. Every swatch here carries its index and the SGR parameter that paints it.

Every mark can be typed.

The logo is characters in monospace cells. The lockup is one printf. If a version cannot be produced by a terminal or pasted into a README, it is not the logo.

Claude's oranges are out.

Terracotta and ginger read as Claude's mark, ruled out for the companion on 31 August 2026 and out for brand accents too. The dawn and dusk horizons stay in the scene, because they are the sky.

Plain words.

Concrete over conceptual, evidence over claim, no clever umbrella terms. The name is lowercase everywhere, including the start of a sentence.

The mark

One terminal cell with the x drawn in reverse video, followed by the rest of the word in the same face. The cell is the text colour of its context; the x is the ground showing through. There is never a third colour in the cell.

Lockup on the dark ground, 233

Lockup on the light ground, 255

xscapes

Same rule reversed: the cell is ink 233, the x is the ground 255.

Geometry

The cell is 0.6 em wide and 1.2 em tall, a ratio of exactly 1:2. Those are Geist Mono 700's advance width and a line height of 1.2, the same cell the terminal draws.

The x sits on the terminal's baseline, 955 units from the top of a 1200-unit cell, which is where a terminal centres the font's ascent and descent. It is not centred on the glyph; that would move it 22 units up and it would stop matching the real cursor.

No tracking. The word is set at the font's own advance, as a terminal would. On the web that means letter-spacing: 0.

Files: the SVGs in assets/brand/ are built from the font's real outlines by make-brand.py, so nothing downstream depends on the font being installed.

Symbol, and the blink

xxOn, and off. The off state is the hollow cell an unfocused window shows. The symbol blinks about twice a second, only when it is the only thing moving on the page. The lockup never blinks.

Clear space and minimum size

xscapes

Clear space is one cell on every side: 0.6 em left and right, 1.2 em above and below. Minimum size for the lockup is 12 px, where the cell is 7 px wide. Below that, use the symbol alone; it holds to 8 px.

Do

  • Set the lockup in Geist Mono 700 with no tracking, and nothing else.
  • Keep the cell the text colour and the x the ground.
  • Keep one cell of clear space around it.
  • Blink the symbol only, and only when nothing else moves.
  • Write it as xscapes everywhere plain text goes.

Do not

  • Recolour the x, or put a third colour in the cell.
  • Set "scapes" in the sans, or in any weight but 700.
  • Stretch, rotate, outline, or add a shadow.
  • Put the mark over the scene. The brand stays off the picture.
  • Capitalise it, even at the start of a sentence, even in a title.

Greys and one accent, on a dark ground first. Every value is a cube entry, so a swatch on a slide is the exact colour on every terminal. Each is named with its index and the SGR parameter that paints it: 38 for text, 48 for a background.

Reverse, the alternate colour application

The same cell filled with gold instead of ink. The x stays the ground and the word stays ink. Nothing else about the mark changes, and none of the rules above relax.

On dark: gold 179

xscapes
printf '\e[48;5;179;38;5;233mx\e[0mscapes'

On light: gold 136 in the cell

xscapes

179 fails against a light ground (1.78:1), so on light the cell takes 136 #af8700, and gold used as text takes 94 #875f00. Both are cube entries; the measured ratios are in the table above.

When

Where the brand is the subject rather than a label: the deck's title and closing slides, the header of the Commons page, the avatar and the social image. It is the warm hand; use it once per surface.

Everywhere the mark labels something else, the cell is ink: section headers, the README, footers, captions. In the terminal it is always plain reverse video, so it takes the user's own colours.

Never

  • Both cells on one surface. A slide has an ink mark or a gold mark, not one of each.
  • Gold on "scapes". Only the cell is gold; the word is always ink.
  • Gold as a text colour on light except 94, and never as a fill behind text.
  • Gold in the sea. The scene keeps its own palette.

Type

Two faces from one family. Geist Mono carries the mark, code, labels and captions; Geist carries everything people read. Both are on Google Fonts under the OFL; the fallbacks are SF Mono or Menlo, and the system sans.

Specimen

Waiting for an agent is dead time.

The sea rises with the work, the sand records it, and the cat knocks when it needs you.

You fire off a prompt, the terminal goes quiet, you tab away, and then you forget to come back. xscapes gives that dead time a face: a small living shoreline that runs with your agent.

go install github.com/donlucasx/xscapes@latest

one go binary · no dependencies · any 256-colour terminal

Voice

Say what it does. Plain over poetic, concrete over conceptual, evidence over claim. Sentences a person would say out loud, with the number in them if there is one.

The first two are the CLI's own help header, so the deck and the binary say the same thing.

Say, and do not say

saynot
a thinking screen for terminal agentsan AI-powered ambient companion experience
the sea rises with the workdelightful real-time activity visualisation
one Go binary, no dependenciesget started in seconds!
measured at 124 by 27, all 48 half-hoursbeautifully smooth gradients

No em dashes, no exclamation marks, no "AI-powered", no "delightful". Numbers go with their units. Nothing is described by how it feels when it can be described by what it does.
