You send a prompt and the terminal goes quiet. A spinner, a verb that changes every few seconds, and nothing else: not whether this is a five-second read or a five-minute refactor, not that a command just failed, not how much context is left before it compacts. So you tab away, and come back late.

Cozy ASCII scenes that react to your agent while it works

A small living scene, drawn in text under your agent's transcript, that makes the wait readable from across the room. The sea rises with the work, the moon shows the context left, and a companion animal knocks when it needs you or is done. Claude Code out of the box; any other terminal agent with one command.

One session, sped up. The agent runs untouched in the top rows. Everything under it is drawn from what it is doing.

{{bg:wait}}
  • ActivitySee how hard it is working, and what it just did. The sea says how much: how many swells are travelling and how tall, whitecaps once it is flat out, flat water when it is waiting on you. The sand says what: every tool call and the file it touched, the newest brightest, the older lines fading as the tide takes them.
  • NotificationsLeave the room. A solid balloon and one sound when it needs an answer, a dotted balloon and a different sound when it is done, and a third when it finished but left something broken. Silent while it works, so every sound you hear is one that matters.
  • SubagentsCount the helpers. A crablet on the sand for each subagent running; when its job is done it walks into the water.
  • FailuresNever miss a failed command. It shows on the companion's face, claws down and amber eyes, until your next prompt.
  • Context meterSee the context running out before it compacts. The moon thins and sinks as the context fills, and from 40% used what is left is written under it as a number.
  • ProgressWatch the work add up. A star for every turn the agent finishes (every todo, when it keeps a list) and one more for every eight subagent jobs, each falling into its place in the sky.
  • Your clockKeep your own hours. The sky is the real time of day where you sit, dawn to night as it happens, so the window tells the hour without a clock and no two sessions look the same.

The companion on its own, in its five states. Press one.

{{bg:get}}

Three commands, no app, no tab, no tmux. It runs in the same window as the agent, so you never look away from the work to see the work.

$ go install github.com/donlucasx/xscapes@latest $ xscapes install claude --apply # writes its hooks, after a backup $ xscapes claude # the agent on top, the scape underneath $ xscapes inside <any command> # anything else, hosted the same way

{{bg:layer}}

The shoreline is the first scape, not the product. Underneath is a small event protocol and a thin adapter for each agent, and any landscape with a light, a sky, something that moves with the work, a surface, somewhere the work piles up and someone living in it carries all of it unchanged. The shoreline and the mountain vista ship today (xscapes scape vista); the rainy window and the aquarium are drawn and next.

Four companions so far, each drawn for its scape. Three ship; a running scape picks a swap up on its next frame.

$ xscapes companion cat

The mountain vista, with the owl. The wind is the work. Still air is a straight column of smoke and upright grass; as the agent gets busy the smoke streams sideways, the scrub flattens and the air fills. The fire lights the meadow at night, and the peaks take the last light at dusk.

The rainy window, with the cat. Rain on the glass is the work: watch it come down harder as the agent gets busy, then ease. The city behind it carries the hour.

The aquarium, with the frog. More fish come out as the work picks up, the tank's lamp is the light, and the gravel is where it settles.

{{bg:month}}

It is not a loop. The sea is your agent's work, the sand is its last few actions and the sky is your clock, so no two sessions draw the same picture. It asks nothing of you, and the agent's own output reaches your terminal byte for byte, so it can never get in the way of the work it is drawing.

Every scene on this page is the real renderer folding the events a Claude Code session emits — text, not video, drawn by the same code that runs in your terminal. Nothing is posed. github.com/donlucasx/xscapes · MIT
